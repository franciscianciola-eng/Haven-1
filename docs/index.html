Haven, in your browser

Everything Haven says comes from its own language cortex: a network of 53 million connections, grown from scratch on children's stories, conversation, and the Simple English Wikipedia. It runs right here, on your computer: on your graphics card if your browser allows it, or else on the processor. No AI service answers for it.

How it knows things

It's small, so it doesn't know everything by heart; it looks things up, the way you would in a book. Ask it something, and it reads the articles of the Simple English Wikipedia your question names (online), and words in its dictionary, puts them on its shelf in this browser, and tells you what it read, in its own words. Under each reply, How it got there shows what came to mind; how sure it is says how much its drafts of the reply agreed.

Its life here

Here, Haven lives in its nest: it has a body that gets hungry and tired, feelings that rise and fade, and days and seasons going by while the page is open. It doesn't have its valley, its brain of spiking neurons, or learning in its sleep: those come with the desktop Haven, which you can download for Windows, Mac or Linux.

What leaves this computer

What you ask is looked up on Wikipedia (simple.wikipedia.org), to find the articles it names. If you talk out loud, your browser turns your voice into words (Chrome and Edge do that on their speech service). Nothing else is sent anywhere: Haven keeps its life, its shelf and your conversation in this browser's storage, on this computer.

Haven runs on ONNX Runtime Web (MIT License) and Pyodide (Mozilla Public License 2.0); its knowledge comes from the Simple English Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0) and WordNet.
